§ I

Three sentences, so nobody has to guess.

Most portfolios open with a hero image and a job title. This one owes you a straight answer first — what follows is unusual, and your time is not free.

The first

It is a portfolio.

Ten projects that shipped. Every demo runs live — not a screenshot, not a video. Every project names one real thing that went wrong, because a career where nothing broke is a career that did not happen.

The second

It is also a world.

Nineteen places, sixteen puzzles, and a guardian made of light that remembers your last visit. Everything you recover repairs a piece of the world — you can watch the forest come back as you read.

The third

You never have to play it.

One key opens the entire archive at any moment, from anywhere, with nothing withheld and no penalty. If you are here to check a stack and leave, that is a legitimate visit and it is designed for.

§ II

Who did this, and why

“The parts nobody photographs — error handling, migration paths, the second week after launch — decide whether software is trustworthy at three in the morning.”

Six years of shipping software other people depend on: scheduling systems, internal tools, and the unglamorous layer underneath a product that decides whether it is trustworthy under load.

I have owned things end to end and I have handed things over cleanly, which is a different and underrated skill. Most of what I am proud of is invisible from the outside.

This site exists because a bullet list never conveyed any of that. So I put the whole thing somewhere you can walk through — and then made very sure you could skip the walk.

Build note · not part of the screen

  • ◈The boot is an exploded view that reassembles. Seventeen parts scatter outward in 3D, then land in engine-assembly order: chassis rail → the block → headline → controls → the four gauges → mounted cards → the tech belt. Each part flashes as it seats. Total 2.4 s, skippable with a click, a key, or a scroll.
  • ◈Scatter is deterministic, not random. Each part's launch vector is computed from its own position relative to screen centre, so parts fly out along their true axis — an exploded diagram, not confetti. The same part lands the same way every reload.
  • ◈Below the fold keeps the language. Every section assembles from depth on scroll (translate + rotateX + scale), so the metaphor holds for the whole page rather than being a one-off intro trick.
  • ◈Compositor-only. transform and opacity exclusively; will-change is set during flight and dropped the moment a part seats, so no permanent GPU layers are left behind.
  • ◈Reduced motion is honoured completely — no explosion, no assembly, everything pre-seated, canvas painted once. Nothing is lost, only stilled.
  • ◈Video slot wired and commented in .stage. 14-second silent loop, 1600×900, ~1.8 MB WebM + MP4, canvas beneath as permanent fallback. Cannot be shot until the engine runs — lands at the end of Phase 1.
  • ◈The cyclist is deliberately behind everything. He sits in the fixed decorative layer at z-index:0; all content is above him, so he can never sit on top of a project card or a contact link. Opacity 0.6, hidden below 960 px, and a real off switch — the toggle bottom-left, the B key, and the choice is remembered in localStorage. A visitor who wants a serious page gets one in a single keypress.
  • ◈He is SVG, not a 3D model. Gradient-shaded body parts with a cyan rim light tie him to the world palette; depth comes from CSS 3D on the container (translateZ + perspective), so he rides closer at mid-screen and further at the edges. Zero dependencies, ~6 KB, no model file, no Three.js on this route. His pedal rate is driven by your scroll velocity — he keeps up with you, and coasts when you stop.
  • ◈The comedy is scoped on purpose. Fourteen dry lines, six crash lines, five tricks, and a console message. Nothing comedic touches the project cards, the metrics, or the “what broke” blocks — those carry the credibility and are left completely straight. Personality in the margins, seriousness in the substance.
  • ◈Replace before shipping: portrait, the quote and two bio paragraphs, email and social handles, skill percentages, and all three project cards. Shapes are right; facts are invented.
